# Phase 9 research direction (Research Director, 2026-10-13)

## What Phase 8 taught us
- Newcomer base rates are about 4–6% in every dataset measured. Winners without an audience over-index on:
  - high price per account
  - small teams
  - paid or partner channels
- Copy speed is about 6 months. Every visible gap the agents found had already been entered. AI-built supply roughly tripled newcomer volume from 2024 to 2026.
- Ideas whose only edge is "first to notice" (H7, N7, N12, M1) decay before they pay back.
- No Phase 8 idea entered the top cluster.

## Phase 9 direction: copy-resistant positions open to this founder
Look for businesses where a newcomer's edge comes from something a fast follower cannot clone in weeks, and where this founder can actually obtain that edge within about 3–9 months with about $2k plus time.

Each opportunity must give evidence of three things:
1. **Spending:** transaction signals.
2. **The moat:**
   - what exactly blocks a copier;
   - how long it took recent entrants to obtain it;
   - how many holders exist and whether that number is growing.
3. **Newcomer outcomes:** for recent holders of that moat.

Use the Phase 8 base rates as the prior. Do not resubmit ledger items.

## Agent assignments
| Agent | Moat type to search |
|---|---|
| A Consumer | Thin-market local network effects and trust assets: local two-sided marketplaces, community registries, review or verification assets in small language markets. Measure how recent entrants fared against incumbents. |
| B Physical | Exclusive or contracted supply, rights or certifications: exclusive import or distribution rights obtainable by a small firm, certified products (CE, medical, food) where certification is the barrier, licensed IP for physical goods. Measure the time and cost to certify, and holder counts. |
| C Traditional | Licences, registrations and accredited statuses a foreign founder or Israeli entity can obtain in months, with a regulated monopoly on a paid task, in Israel or abroad. Include buying small businesses that hold contracts or licences, with Israeli or EU listing data. |
| D Software | Software moats beyond "first": certified integrations, such as government e-invoicing or tax-authority certified software, bank or payment certification, and app-store categories requiring approval. Also proprietary data accrued through operation, and switching-cost B2B tools. Measure certification lead times and holder counts, for example certified software lists published by tax authorities. |
| E Distribution | Contracted or exclusive distribution: preferred-vendor contracts, framework agreements, association endorsements and white-label deals that block competitors for a term. Check how newcomers obtained them. |
| F Contrarian | Israel-specific structural advantages that foreigners cannot copy quickly, such as Hebrew plus Israeli regulation or institutions, physical presence, or Israeli-only data. Test honestly whether Israel's market is too small, and whether earlier phases' Israel-niche results generalise. Also try to falsify the direction: do moated small businesses actually earn more for newcomers? |
