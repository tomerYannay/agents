# Phase 9, Discovery Agent B (Physical products): copy-resistant supply, certification and licensed-IP positions

Agent label: P9-B. Date: 13-Oct-2026. WebSearch count: **6 of 6** (all logged in `search-log.txt`). Most of the evidence comes from public registry APIs, which were not capped. Scripts and data are in the scratchpad (`p6plus/P9-B/`). Personal fields (names, phones, emails) were removed or never saved.

## Headline finding

I measured the moat directly for every candidate, using holder counts in public registries. Most physical-product "certification moats" open to a $2k founder have **already been removed by regulation or are crowded**:

| Moat (registry) | Time and cost to obtain | Holders | Trend | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Israel MoH medical devices (AMAR), **Class I declaration route** | Free; confirmation within 48 h; yearly renewal | 1,161 active holders across all classes; 137 holders have *only* declaration-route registrations | 49–55% of the newest holder cohorts' registrations are declaration-route | **No moat** |
| AMAR **Class II/IIa fast track** | 45 business days (Emergo) or 14–60 days (meddeviceguide); IRH must hold a QMS certificate (ISO 9001) | 16–50 active holders per home-use category | About 6 new holders per category since about 2023 | **Thin moat** (best of the set) |
| Israel MoEP **household pesticides** | "Lengthy, complex" toxicity and efficacy review; form is free of charge | **~25 holders**, ~157 parseable products | Registration numbers reach only ~689 in total; roughly 10 new per year [Hypothesis] | **Strong moat, but cost and time unknown** |
| Israel MoH **cosmetics**, EU track (since Jan-2025) | Notification | 843 importers; **434 appear only on the EU track** | 37,513 EU-track notifications versus 24,478 basic-track ones in all prior years | **Moat collapsed** |
| Israel MoH **food importer** registration | Registration certificate | 3,098 valid holders | 1,574 hold numbers ≥15,000 (recent) | **No moat** |
| Israel MoC **wireless commercial approval** | Per model | 2,804 models on the list usable for "approval based on approval" | Piggyback route exists | **No moat** |
| **EU MDR Class I** (EUDAMED) | Self-certified technical file | 32,592 manufacturer actors; **1,996,732 Class I UDI-DI records** | Large and growing | **No moat** |
| **US FDA** device establishment | **$13,785/yr** fee (FY2027) | n/a | n/a | **Over budget (6.9×)** |
| Israeli mandatory standards **without an EU route** | Israel Standards Institute (SII) testing per model | 13 of 44 mandatory standards: fire extinguishers, electric water heaters, samovars, gas cookers, playgrounds, lifts | Shrinking list | **Moat real, cost [Unknown] and probably above budget** |

**Implication for the Phase 9 direction.** For physical goods, regulatory moats that a newcomer can get in 3–9 months with $2k are exactly the ones Israel's 2023–2025 "what's good for Europe" reforms flattened. The moats that remain (household pesticides, SII-tested categories, Class IIb and above) have costs or timelines I could not verify. Those costs are probably above budget. Only one idea, B1, reaches a defensible-but-thin position. Nothing here clearly beats the Phase 8 newcomer base rate of about 4–6%.

## Opportunities (ranked)

### B1. Fast-track (Class II/IIa) Israeli registration holder for one niche home-use device, with written manufacturer exclusivity, sold DTC in Hebrew
1. **Concept.** Pick a home-use Class IIa (EU) or Class II (FDA) device that is CE- or FDA-cleared and has few Israeli holders, for example wearable breast pumps, OTC-style hearing amplifiers or aids, LED light-therapy masks, or iontophoresis devices. Sign an exclusive Israel authorisation with a small manufacturer. Register it via the AMAR fast track and sell DTC plus pharmacy or baby-chain wholesale. Unlike Class I, a copier needs the manufacturer's letter, a QMS certificate and a fast-track filing.
2. **Customer.** Israeli consumers (for example, new mothers), plus pharmacies and specialist stores.
3. **Model.** One-off product sales at ₪400–1,000, plus consumables (flanges, gel pads, electrodes) [Hypothesis].
4. **Spending evidence.**
   - Zap lists 98 nursing and feeding results for "משאבת חלב". There are multiple electric pumps at ₪300–2,526, and Biamba wearable pumps sell at ₪428–999 [Verified: zap.co.il search, 13-Oct-2026]. These are listings, not sales counts.
   - A recent holder registered a Chinese wearable breast pump in 2026, expiring 2031 [Verified: MoH registry API, 13-Oct-2026].
   - No unit-sales data. Transaction signals are **weak**.
5. **Competitors.**
   - Active holders per category [Verified: MoH registry API, 13-Oct-2026]:

     | Category | Active holders | Recent holders (code ≥3750, about 2023+) |
     |---|---|---|
     | Breast pumps | 16 | 2 |
     | Hearing aids | 28 | 6 |
     | BP monitors | 50 | 4 |
     | Pulse oximeters | 29 | 2 |
     | Light-therapy/LED | 74 | 8 |

   - Incumbents are multi-brand medical importers. The top 50 holders hold 46.1% of devices [Verified: meddeviceguide.com, 13-Jun-2026].
   - What customers dislike: [Unknown]. No review data was found.
6. **Entry strategy.**
   - Choose a manufacturer with **no current Israeli holder**, check it against the registry API (5,580 active manufacturers), and sign exclusivity.
   - Caveat: **19.7% of active manufacturers already have more than one Israeli holder, and 33% (303/927) of Chinese manufacturers do** [Verified: own analysis of the MoH registry, 13-Oct-2026]. Exclusivity from generic Chinese OEMs is therefore not reliable. Brand-owning EU or US makers are the realistic exclusivity partners.
7. **Acquisition.**
   - Meta and Instagram ads in Hebrew, plus parenting influencers. Paid channels carry a 3–6× lift in the Phase 8 TrustMRR data, but that is software. **No newcomer evidence** for Israeli medical-device DTC.
   - Recent-holder outcomes are poor. In the holder cohorts that entered about 2016–2021 (codes 3000–3499), only **47–49% still hold any active registration**. **Median active registrations per recent holder = 1** [Verified: own analysis, 13-Oct-2026].
8. **Capital, operations and legality.**
   - The IRH needs an Israeli entity and a QMS certificate. Meddeviceguide says ISO 9001; the Emergo 2023 article says "QMS certificate for importer and manufacturer" [Verified: emergobyul.com, 18-Sep-2023; meddeviceguide.com, 13-Jun-2026].
   - ISO 9001 cost in Israel: **[Unknown]**. One search found no Israeli price. Likely several thousand dollars plus annual audits [Hypothesis]. **Flag: probably above $2k** with first inventory included (MOQ of 100 units at $30–60 is about $3–6k) [Hypothesis].
   - Fast-track review: 45 business days (Fast 1) [Secondary: Emergo snippet]; meddeviceguide says 60 days with one 510(k), or 14 days with two authorisations [Verified, 13-Jun-2026]. **Moat time for a copier: about 2–4 months plus the QMS.**
   - Israeli operability is good.
9. **Labour.** Delegable after setup (3PL plus ads). Regulatory upkeep covers 5-year validity, changes and vigilance [Verified: 5-year max, Secondary snippet].
10. **Unit economics [Hypothesis].**
    - At ₪600 retail and ₪150 landed cost, contribution is ₪250–350 after VAT, ads and fulfilment, so CAC must stay below about ₪200.
    - Track A needs about 60–80 units a month. Track B is not credible on a single SKU, but possible as a stacked portfolio of 5–10 exclusive devices.
11. **Risks.**
    - Personal-import leakage: consumers buy unregistered devices on AliExpress. Enforcement exists, for example the MoEP and MoH warnings after unregistered imports [Verified for pesticides: ice.co.il, 3-Jul-2024].
    - The manufacturer can terminate exclusivity, and another holder can register the same model.
    - Device rules on parallel import: **[Unknown]**. One search found none.
    - **Decisive unverified assumption:** Israeli DTC demand for one niche device reaches 60+ units a month at a CAC under ₪200.

### B2. Israeli household-pesticide registration for a proven foreign product (B2B to licensed exterminators, then retail)
1. **Concept.** Register an EU- or US-registered product that has no Israeli registration, for example a biological larvicide, bed-bug or fire-ant products, or a novel mosquito device. Sell it to Israel's licensed exterminators, then to retail.
2. **Customer.** 2,153 licensed exterminators (records in the MoEP licensed-exterminator dataset, aggregate only) [Verified: data.gov.il `madbirim`, 13-Oct-2026]. Later, households via chains.
3. **Model.** Wholesale with consumable reorders.
4. **Spending evidence.**
   - The incumbents are real companies: SANO holds 24 registrations and Rimi Chemicals 22 [Verified: data.gov.il `domestic_pesticides`, 13-Oct-2026].
   - Category spend and sales: [Unknown]. **Weak.**
5. **Competitors and moat.**
   - **Only about 25 holders.** Registration numbers run only up to about 689 since the registry began, with the highest recent run at 678–689.
   - One registration holder appears to be an individual (a person's name, withheld here), so small holders are possible.
   - The process is "lengthy and complex" (toxicity, safety, efficacy) [Verified: ice.co.il, 3-Jul-2024]. The form is free of charge and reviewed by an inter-ministerial committee [Secondary: gov.il service-page search snippet; gov.il returned 403].
   - This is the **strongest moat measured**, but the time to obtain it is **[Unknown]**, and is likely 12+ months and needs local efficacy data [Hypothesis].
6. **Entry.** Pick a product class absent from the registry (the registry lists purpose and active ingredient, so gaps can be enumerated), and sign exclusivity with a foreign maker that already holds BPR or EPA data.
7. **Acquisition.** An enumerable licensed-exterminator base (2,153), reached by direct outreach and distributors. No newcomer evidence.
8. **Capital.** Fees appear nil, but dossier preparation, local testing (if required) and inventory are [Unknown] and likely above $2k. **Flag.** The timeline probably exceeds 9 months.
9. **Labour.** Low after registration.
10. **Economics [Hypothesis].** B2B chemical margins of 30–50% are plausible. The market is small, so this is Track A at best.
11. **Risks.** Registration may never be granted. Small Israeli market, and incumbent price response. **Decisive unverified assumption:** a foreign-registered product can be registered in Israel within 9 months without local efficacy trials.

### B3. Israeli Registration Holder (IRH) portfolio for foreign DTC device brands (rights held, not dev hours)
1. **Concept.** Act as the IRH for 10–30 small foreign home-use device brands that sell online to Israel, for example through Amazon.com, which ships to Israel, or their own Shopify stores. Hold their registrations, handle vigilance, and earn an annual fee or revenue share. The registration sits in the holder's name, so a brand that wants to switch holders must go through MoH's transfer procedure [Secondary: gov.il form title "העברת רישום" in search results for pesticides; device transfer rule [Unknown]].
2. **Customer.** Foreign SMEs, including Chinese, EU and US makers.
3. **Model.** A setup fee plus an annual retainer per registration [Hypothesis].
4. **Spending evidence.**
   - 223 holders had a declaration-route registration as their first filing, so they entered after Sep-2023 [Verified: own analysis, 13-Oct-2026].
   - A firm named "BIOREG SERVICES" holds a 2030 registration for a US manufacturer's test kit, which shows the service model exists [Verified: MoH registry API].
   - Consultancies such as Emergo and Pure Global sell Israeli registration [Secondary: search results].
   - Prices: [Unknown].
5. **Competitors.** Global RA consultancies and Israeli regulatory firms.
6. **Entry.** An English and Chinese self-serve portal plus automation of the annual declaration-route renewals, which cost nothing but are mandatory each year [Verified: Emergo, 18-Sep-2023]. **This is a service.** Agent C's licences remit overlaps.
7. **Acquisition.** Outbound to the manufacturers that EUDAMED and openFDA list with no Israeli holder (enumerable). No evidence on conversion.
8. **Capital.** Needs a QMS certificate ([Unknown] cost; flag) and product liability exposure.
9. **Labour.** Per-file work. Scalable only if automated.
10. **Economics [Hypothesis].** 20 clients at $1,500 a year is about $2.5k a month, so Track A at the low end.
11. **Risks.** Liability as the legal holder; price competition from consultancies. **Decisive unverified assumption:** foreign SMEs will pay a recurring IRH fee when Class I registration itself is free.

### B4. Import of an Israel-specific SII-tested category (no EU route), for example fire extinguishers
1. **Concept.** 13 of the 44 still-mandatory Israeli standards have no EU or CASSIS import route. They include **SI 66, 318, 463, 987 and 1153 (portable and disposable fire extinguishers)**, SI 69 (electric water heaters), SI 251 (electric samovars) and SI 1049 (domestic gas-electric cookers) [Verified: data.gov.il `mandatory-standards` reform table, 13-Oct-2026]. A per-model SII test blocks casual copiers.
2. **Customer.** Households and businesses (extinguishers), and religious households (samovars, an affinity niche the brief warns against).
3. **Model.** Product sales.
4. **Spending evidence.** [Unknown]. Not investigated within the search cap.
5. **Competitors.** Local manufacturers and established importers [Hypothesis].
6. **Entry.** None credible without price data.
7. **Acquisition.** [Unknown].
8. **Capital.** SII test fees [Unknown], probably above $2k. **Flag.**
9. **Labour.** Low.
10. **Economics.** [Unknown].
11. **Risks.** A standards reform may add an EU route later (the list is shrinking), which would erase the moat. **Decisive unverified assumption:** SII testing for one disposable-extinguisher model costs under about $2k. It is listed for completeness; I would not pursue it.

### B5. Exclusive Israeli licence for a foreign consumer IP (character, brand or design) on physical goods
1. **Concept.** Take an exclusive territorial licence from a small foreign IP owner (indie game, webcomic or design brand) for Hebrew-market physical goods.
2. **Spending evidence.** None found.
3. **Terms.** General terms only: advances are 25–50% of the minimum guarantee [Secondary: uschamber.com, via search]; royalty bands are 5–8% for toys and 6–10% for apparel [Secondary: enhancepd.com, patent-focused].
4. **Newcomer outcomes.** No small-licensee outcome data. The closest earlier evidence is Makeship (P7, M7 killed) and board-game localisation (P6, excluded).
5. **Moat.** The licence is exclusive, but fan demand is global and parallel import of the licensor's own goods is legal [Hypothesis].
6. **Entry.** None credible. Ranked last and effectively a kill.

## Killed ideas (with measured evidence)
- **Class I declaration-route private label in Israel** (pimple patches, scar sheets, cast covers, supports).
  - Registration is free and arrives in 48 h with yearly renewal [Verified: Emergo, 18-Sep-2023].
  - Small DTC firms already do this: recent holders registered Chinese hydrocolloid patches, cast covers, silicone scar sheets and orthopaedic supports in 2024–26 [Verified: MoH registry API]. Zap shows Kamedis pimple patches at ₪38–53 [Verified: zap.co.il, 13-Oct-2026].
  - **Not a moat.** It is the Phase 8 "first to notice" trap.
- **EU MDR Class I own brand.**
  - EUDAMED lists 32,592 manufacturer actors and **1,996,732 Class I UDI-DI records out of 4,106,723** [Verified: ec.europa.eu/tools/eudamed/api, 13-Oct-2026].
  - Israeli manufacturers in EUDAMED: 395 (registered 2021: 126; 2026: 17). This is an onboarding curve, not market entry.
- **US FDA-registered own-label devices.** The establishment registration fee is **$13,785 for FY2027** [Verified: fda.gov MDUFA fees page, 13-Oct-2026], 6.9× the budget.
- **Israeli cosmetics import or own brand.**
  - 61,991 notifications: 37,513 on the EU track (since Jan-2025) versus 24,478 on the basic track. 434 importers appear only on the EU track [Verified: registries.health.gov.il cosmetics API, 13-Oct-2026].
  - A parallel-import track without manufacturer documents has started with 15 products [Secondary: gov.il and news search results].
  - The moat collapsed.
- **Israeli food, alcohol or pet-food import registration.**
  - 3,098 valid importers: 2,467 regular, 385 alcohol, 209 veterinary [Verified: data.gov.il `fcs-importers`, 13-Oct-2026].
  - 51% hold registration numbers ≥15,000 (recent).
  - Low barrier, so no moat.
- **Israeli wireless (MoC) type approvals.** 2,804 models sit on the list of approvals valid for "approval based on approval" filings by other importers [Verified: data.gov.il `items-appproved-by-personal-import`, 13-Oct-2026]. Copiers piggyback, so no moat.
- **Exclusive EU importer for small US brands under GPSR/EPR.** Too close to the ledger's "first distributor in CEE/LatAm" (P6) and "UK/EU commission agent" (P7). It was not tested further.
- **Hearing-aid OTC DTC in Israel.**
  - 28 active holders, 6 recent [Verified: MoH registry API].
  - Zap shows almost no hearing-aid devices, only batteries and amplifiers [Verified: zap.co.il, 13-Oct-2026].
  - The channel is audiologist-led, and whether Israeli law requires fitting is [Unknown]. Dropped early.

## Ranking and diligence
1. **B1** (fast-track device exclusivity). This is the only idea with a measurable moat that a newcomer can obtain in 2–4 months. The cohort data are discouraging: about half of holders lapse, and the median holder keeps one registration. QMS plus inventory probably exceeds $2k.
2. **B2** (household-pesticide registration). This is the strongest moat (about 25 holders and very slow growth), but cost and timeline are unverified and probably outside 9 months and $2k.
3. **B3** (IRH portfolio). The holder count grows about 80 a year [Hypothesis from holder-code bands] and the model is low-capital, but it is a service and competes with consultancies.
4. B4 and B5: not recommended.

**Top 2 for deep diligence and their decisive unverified assumptions:**
- **B1:** a single niche Class IIa home device can reach 60+ DTC units a month in Israel at a CAC under ₪200. Secondary: the cost of ISO 9001 for a one-person IRH.
- **B2:** a foreign-registered household pesticide can be registered by MoEP within 9 months without local efficacy trials, at under $2k in dossier cost.

Honest prior: none of these is clearly better than the Phase 8 newcomer base rate of 4–6%. B1 and B2 trade the "copy in 6 months" problem for capital, timeline and demand uncertainty. For physical products, the clearest Phase 9 result is negative. The registries show that Israel's reforms have turned most certification barriers into 48-hour or notification-only steps.
