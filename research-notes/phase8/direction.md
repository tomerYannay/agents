# Phase 8 research direction (Research Director, 2026-10-13)

## What Phases 5–7 taught us
- Newcomer outcomes measured on ~30 channels (marketplaces, communities, procurement, partner directories, co-ops, voucher marketplaces, AI-agent registries). Channels reward incumbents, audience owners and referenced suppliers; newcomers without those get near zero.
- Every Phase 1–5 leader except H7 was re-checked and lowered. The board is flat (#2–#15 within 3.6 points).
- The only verified audience-free newcomer success found so far (Conductor, QuickBooks Desktop API, $42k MRR from a solo founder, 4 Product Hunt upvotes) came from a PATTERN — developers searching for a missing capability — not from a channel we guessed.
- Guessing channels and then testing them has hit diminishing returns.

## Phase 8 direction: "reverse-engineer verified newcomer winners"
Instead of proposing ideas and then looking for evidence, each agent starts from a dataset of RECENT entrants (founded/launched 2023–2026) whose outcomes can be verified from public data, identifies the ones that reached meaningful revenue WITHOUT a pre-existing audience, and extracts replicable patterns (customer, problem type, channel, pricing, why it worked). Then propose concrete opportunities that apply those patterns and are not in the ledger. Agents must report base rates (how many entrants in the dataset vs how many winners) to avoid survivorship bias, and must cover non-software categories where possible.

## Agent assignments (datasets)
- A Consumer: Apple App Store via the public iTunes Search/Lookup API — non-game consumer/prosumer apps first released 2024–2026 with large US rating counts; who made them (indie vs studio vs brand), category, monetisation, and whether growth was paid/creator/brand-driven.
- B Physical: hardware and physical-product newcomers with public outcomes — Crowd Supply and other crowdfunding pages (funded amounts, dates), Shopify-store brands launched 2023–26 with public outcome signals, Empire Flippers/Flippa e-commerce businesses <3 years old with verified P&L.
- C Traditional/offline/services: Empire Flippers public API and Flippa public listings — businesses under ~3 years old with verified profit in NON-content models (services, lead gen, e-commerce, local, B2B); plus any public registries/outcome data for young service firms.
- D Software: TrustMRR systematic mining — startups founded 2024+ with Stripe-verified MRR ≥ $2–3k; classify founder audience (follower counts where shown), model (integration/infra, vertical B2B, consumer, AI wrapper, data), price point; compute the audience-free subset and its shared traits.
- E Distribution: acquisition channels of verified newcomer winners — Indie Hackers product pages with Stripe-verified revenue, founder interviews with verifiable numbers, Product Hunt launch stats vs outcomes; which channels newcomers without audiences actually used (SEO, developer docs, cold outbound, integration directories, communities, paid ads).
- F Contrarian: base rates and negative space — compute success base rates by model across accessible datasets; look for models/geographies where newcomers OUTPERFORM (e.g., non-English markets, legacy-system integrations, B2B "boring" tools); also test whether the Conductor pattern generalises (other legacy desktop/closed systems with integration demand and no self-serve API).
