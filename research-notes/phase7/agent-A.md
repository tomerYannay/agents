# Phase 7 — Discovery Agent A (Consumer & Prosumer, B2B2C / borrowed distribution)

Date: 2026-10-13 · Search-log label: P7-A · WebSearches used: **8 of 8** (one wasted: query 8 returned European Space Agency and GSA results) · WebFetch/curl used on primary pages: comptroller.texas.gov, educationfreedom.texas.gov, abc15.com, edchoice.org, outschool.com, zentrale-pruefstelle-praevention.de (FAQ and certification pages), vdek.com, zm-online.de, krankenkasseninfo.de, gesundheitskurs.de, medizen.digital, fitnessraum.de, gesetze-im-internet.de, perkspot.com, benefithub.com, truemed.com, mako.co.il, nicheacademy.com, and the iTunes Search API.

## Summary in one paragraph

Most "B2B2C" channels in my brief turned out to lend the founder a **budget, not distribution**. Employer perks networks, ESA (education savings account) marketplaces, and HSA/FSA checkout eligibility all let an end-consumer pay with someone else's money. In none of them does the partner push a new supplier's product to its members. Where vendor-level data exists (Arizona ESA), spending concentrates on giants: Amazon took about two-thirds of marketplace spending and Outschool about 40% of online-learning spending. The one channel that does both is Germany's **§20 SGB V prevention-course system**:
- Statutory insurers reimburse 80–100% of a certified course, typically €75–150, about two courses per insured person per year.
- Certification is free and centralised, and certified courses enter a central database that the insurers draw on.
- Employers can add up to €600 per employee per year tax-free for the same certified courses (§3 Nr. 34 EStG).
- A 2019 university spin-off (medizen) sells €149 online courses and shows 1,788 reviews. That is a modest newcomer signal.

The catch: supply is huge (about 115,000 certified offers from about 65,000 providers), and whether foreign-language online courses (IKT, Chapter 5) can be certified is the decisive unknown. I rank it first, ahead of an ESA-funded special-needs online programme. Everything else is weak or killed.

## A1. Insurer-reimbursed online prevention courses for Germany's non-German-speaking insured (§20 SGB V, ZPP-certified IKT courses)

1. **Concept.** Build and certify 8–12-unit online prevention courses (exercise or stress management) delivered in Russian, Ukrainian, Turkish or Arabic. The consumer pays about €99–149, the insurer reimburses 80–100%, and the same certificate qualifies for employer-funded health budgets.
2. **Paying customer and geography.** People insured by German statutory insurers (GKV). The consumer pays upfront and the Krankenkasse refunds. Secondary payer: German employers using the tax-free €600 per employee per year.
3. **Model and price.**
   - One-off course fee, benchmarked at €149 per course (medizen lists three online courses at "149 € Krankenkasse zahlt*") [Verified: medizen.digital, accessed 2026-10-13].
   - TK reimburses online courses "bis zu 100 Euro (bei 100-prozentiger Kostenübernahme)". Typical caps are €75–150 per course, with usually two courses per year [Verified: krankenkasseninfo.de, published 2026-08-03]. A €149 price therefore exceeds some insurers' caps, so pricing at about €99–100 may be what maximises refunds [Hypothesis].
   - Repeat purchase is possible: two courses per year across four fields (exercise, nutrition, stress, addictive substances).
4. **Spending evidence (transaction signals).**
   - Almost 1.9 million insured people used a subsidised prevention measure in 2024, up about 300,000 on the year before [Verified: vdek press release, 2026-01-21].
   - "Etwa 73 Prozent der Kurse wurden in Präsenz durchgeführt, 27 Prozent fanden online statt." Exercise was 63% of participations and stress 31% [Verified: zm-online.de, 2025-11-27, reporting Präventionsbericht 2025, data year 2024].
   - Insurers spent over €243M on individual prevention courses in 2025 [Secondary: vdek/AOK press statements via search snippet, not opened]. A rough online share is 27% × about 1.9M ≈ 500k online participations per year [Hypothesis: share may refer to courses, not participants].
   - **Newcomer-ish signal.** medizen is a Ruhr-Universität Bochum project, EU-funded, with its chair awarded in 2019. It sells €149 online courses and shows "4.9 basierend auf 1.788 Bewertungen" [Verified: medizen.digital, 2026-10-13]. It is university-backed, so not a pure solo newcomer.
   - On the gesundheitskurs.de marketplace (umove GmbH, Berlin), small third-party coaches (Valitudo, FitnessRAUM, Wellfy, Die Vorturner, an individual trainer) list courses. Unlabelled per-course counts range from 82 to 1,012. The site shows 5,294 ratings at 4.7 [Verified: gesundheitskurs.de, 2026-10-13; counts unlabelled, so treat as weak].
5. **Competitors and dislikes.**
   - **Platforms:** gesundheitskurs.de (75+ certified online courses, all in German on the page I saw), FitnessRAUM (2,500 courses in a subscription library; ZPP courses as a lead magnet with "20% Rabatt + Fitness-Geschenk … 49 Euro"), medizen, and many in-person providers.
   - **Large apps:** Gymondo and YogaEasy probably hold certified courses [Unknown: not checked].
   - **Supply:** about 115,000 certified offers from about 65,000 providers, and about 45,000 offers certified in 2025 alone [Verified: vdek, 2026-01-21].
   - **Dislikes:** not measured. Possible ones: the 100%-attendance rule for online courses, reimbursement caps below the price, and German-only content [Hypothesis].
6. **Entry strategy.**
   - **Language segment.** No foreign-language online prevention course surfaced in my search, and gesundheitskurs.de showed none, but absence of search results ≠ absence. Germany has large Russian-, Turkish-, Ukrainian- and Arabic-speaking GKV populations [Unknown: sizes not verified this phase].
   - **Rules, as verified:**
     - The ZPP FAQ explicitly permits Chapter 7 digital offers in foreign languages with a translator's confirmation. Chapter 7, however, needs a registered study protocol, a 3-point evidence study and a data-protection impact assessment (DSFA).
     - The FAQ is **silent** on languages for Chapter 5 IKT courses, which have lighter rules: 8–12 units of 45–90 minutes, reactive exchange with a course leader, 1-year certification, then an evaluation to extend to 3 years [Verified: ZPP FAQ, accessed 2026-10-13].
   - Fallback wedge if languages are blocked: under-served German-language niches such as shift workers, pregnancy and postpartum, or sedentary IT staff. Some of these already exist (pelvic-floor and pregnancy yoga are on gesundheitskurs.de), so this wedge is weak.
7. **Acquisition mechanism and channel evidence.**
   - **Borrowed discoverability.** ZPP says a certified course "erscheint in der zentrale[n Datenbank]" and reaches "über 74 Millionen Versicherte" [Verified: ZPP certification page]. Insurers' course-search tools draw on it.
   - **Paid and affiliate.** Deal sites: a mydealz post advertised a medizen course at "100% Kostenübernahme" plus a free steel bottle [Verified: mydealz page, expired deal; no votes visible]. Free-gift incentives are the competitive norm (FitnessRAUM's €49 goodie).
   - **Language-community channels.** Russian, Ukrainian and Turkish Facebook/Telegram groups in Germany and community organisations [Hypothesis].
   - **Employer channel.** Same certificate under §3 Nr. 34 EStG, "soweit sie 600 Euro im Kalenderjahr nicht übersteigen" [Verified: gesetze-im-internet.de]. Corporate fitness networks reportedly carry ZPP courses (Hansefit hosts ZPP certificates) [Anecdote: search result only; PDF unreadable].
   - **Newcomer outcomes.** Not measured per course. There is no public per-course participation data, and the gesundheitskurs.de numbers are unlabelled. CAC [Unknown].
8. **Capital, operations and legal.**
   - Certification is free and takes about 10 working days [Verified: ZPP page]. The course leader must be a ZPP-recognised professional (e.g., sports scientist, physiotherapist, psychologist); the founder is not one.
   - Build cost: an LMS or app with module gating, progress logging and a messaging channel; video production in one language. About $2k is plausible only with a co-creator trainer on revenue share [Hypothesis].
   - GDPR declaration required.
   - **Israel-based operation [Unknown].** Nothing seen requires a German seat. Invoices, VAT treatment and course-leader location need checking.
9. **Labour dependence.** Content is recorded once. The per-participant load is reactive Q&A and certificate issuing, which can be automated or delegated to the trainer. Each topic and language is a fixed-cost asset, so revenue can grow faster than founder hours. Annual evaluation reports are a recurring obligation.
10. **Unit economics (rough).**
    - About €100–149 gross per participant, minus about 3% payment fees, about €10–20 trainer share or support, and CAC [Unknown].
    - If CAC stays near €30–50 (deal-site or community driven), contribution is about €40–90 per participant [Hypothesis].
    - **Track A:** about 50–80 participants per month → about €2–5k profit per month [Hypothesis].
    - **Track B:** needs about 300+ per month across several languages and topics plus employer deals. That is plausible only if languages are open and uncontested [Hypothesis].
11. **Risks and kill conditions.**
    - Chapter 5 IKT courses must be German-language (the decisive unknown).
    - Insurer caps fall, or online courses are de-prioritised.
    - The cost of a qualified foreign-language course leader is too high.
    - Large apps add language versions.
    - The 1-year evaluation fails.
    - Competitors' gift incentives drive CAC above €60.

## A2. ESA-funded specialist online programme for IEP/neurodivergent students (Texas TEFA first, then Arizona, Florida and other ESA states)

1. **Concept.** An approved ESA-marketplace vendor offering a structured small-group online programme, for example structured literacy for dyslexia or executive-function coaching. Families pay with state ESA funds, and IEP students carry the largest awards.
2. **Paying customer and geography.** US families in ESA states. The money is the state's, routed through Odyssey (TX) or ClassWallet-type platforms.
3. **Model and price.** Per-term packages or hourly small-group sessions; price benchmark [Unknown] (not fetched). Award sizes:
   - **Texas homeschool:** $2,000 per year paid upfront.
   - **Texas private school:** first instalment $10,474.
   - **Texas IEP:** up to $30,000 [Verified: outschool.com/esa/approved-list, updated 2026-08-25; Comptroller PR 2026-06-30].
   - **Arizona:** average about $7,500 [Outschool page; vendor claim].
4. **Spending evidence.**
   - **TEFA:** nearly 73,000 accounts funded on 1 July 2026; "more than 54,000 approved educational products and services from nearly 2,400 vendors"; vendors "will not be charged fees to list, buy or sell" [Verified: comptroller.texas.gov, 2026-06-30]. More than 274,000 applications and over 100,000 awards; SB 2 provided $1B [Verified: educationfreedom.texas.gov, 2026-10-13].
   - **Arizona:** $661M over 4 years and 2.6M transactions, 37% through the marketplace. Online learning was $30M, of which Outschool took $12M. Computers and technology were $60M [Verified: abc15.com, date not shown]. EdChoice reports 100,000 families and over $1B in 2025-26 [Verified: edchoice.org, published 2026-04-01, corrected 2026-07-23].
5. **Competitors and dislikes.** Amazon took about $164M of Arizona marketplace spending (about two-thirds). Then come Apple, Outschool, Staples and Lakeshore. Outschool is ESA-approved in 16 states. Dyslexia programmes and Orton-Gillingham tutors exist widely [Unknown: not enumerated]. Dislikes not measured.
6. **Entry strategy.** The IEP segment, with the largest per-child budgets, and a specialist programme rather than generic classes. Also multi-state approval, since online and out-of-state vendors are allowed in Texas "if the provider applies for approval and is accepted" [Verified: TEFA providers page]. This differentiation is modest. I do not see a strong price or language gap.
7. **Acquisition.**
   - Marketplace listing alone is a catalogue among about 54,000 products, so it is **not credited as distribution**.
   - Realistic channels: ESA parent Facebook groups, homeschool co-ops, special-ed advocates, and Outschool itself (which takes a fee; not verified this phase).
   - **Newcomer outcomes [Unknown].** My dedicated search found nothing. Outschool listing data is client-rendered GraphQL, which I did not scrape.
8. **Capital, operations and legal.**
   - Vendors "must have the right to transact business in Texas" [Verified]. That means a US LLC plus Texas foreign registration (cost not verified).
   - Tutors must be US-based or at least credentialed. Expect background checks and audits by the Comptroller, an independent auditor and the State Auditor.
   - Delivery needs hired tutors.
   - **Policy risk.** ESAs are politically contested, and Arizona has an audit controversy (20% vs 2% unallowable).
9. **Labour.** A tutoring agency. Revenue scales with tutor hours, so the founder's hours can be delegated but margins are capped. A software-led programme (adaptive practice plus a weekly live group) would improve leverage [Hypothesis].
10. **Economics.** If a small group of 4 pays $40 per child-hour and the tutor costs $35 per hour, gross is about $125 per session hour [Hypothesis]. **Track A** is plausible with about 40–60 enrolled children. **Track B** requires a multi-state operation.
11. **Kill conditions.** Marketplace discovery is dominated by Amazon and Outschool. Payment and audit friction. Program repeal or funding caps. Tutor credentialing required per state.

## A3. HSA/FSA-eligible wellness product sold through Truemed-style eligibility checkout (US)

1. **Concept.** A physical or digital wellness product (e.g., a programme bundled with a device) that customers pay for with pre-tax HSA/FSA money via a letter of medical necessity (LMN) integrated at checkout.
2. **Customer.** US consumers with HSA/FSA balances.
3. **Model.** The product price is paid with pre-tax money, roughly a 20–40% effective discount for the consumer [Hypothesis]. The merchant fee to Truemed is [Unknown]: the page does not state it.
4. **Evidence.** "Over 2 Million Americans Shop with Truemed". The directory shows "thousands of partner brands", including small brands (Heart & Soil, Bonafide, Clove, VKTRY, Sans, Stoko). "90% of LMNs are issued within 7 hours" [Verified: truemed.com, 2026-10-13; vendor claims].
5. **Competitors.** Every DTC wellness brand can add the same integration, so it is not a moat.
6. **Entry strategy.** None credible on its own. It is a conversion booster, not a channel.
7. **Acquisition.** Truemed's brand directory gives some borrowed discoverability, but newcomer outcomes are [Unknown]. Acquisition is otherwise your own DTC.
8. **Operations.** Product-dependent. Regulatory risk: the IRS has publicly warned about improper LMN-based eligibility claims [Secondary, recollection; not verified this phase].
9–10. Depends on the product; not an independent business.
11. **Kill condition.** IRS enforcement against LMN marketing. **Verdict:** a feature, not an opportunity. Keep as an add-on to any US wellness product.

## A4. Digital enrichment programme listed in Israel's Ministry of Education Gefen catalogue

1. **Concept.** A school-purchased digital or hybrid enrichment programme (e.g., an AI or coding project curriculum) listed in the Gefen supplier pool so that principals can buy it from their Gefen budgets.
2. **Customer.** Israeli schools; students are the end users.
3. **Model.** A per-class or per-hour programme price paid from the school's Gefen allocation [Unknown: rates not fetched].
4. **Evidence.** The catalogue held "23 אלף תוכניות" from "7,000 ספקים" [Verified: mako.co.il, 2023-08-24]. Search snippets say tender 13/3.2022 reopened for new suppliers in November 2025, with first approvals for 1 year, then 3 years after a further tender [Secondary: search snippets of mr.gov.il / makorrishon, not opened; makorrishon returned 403]. The total Gefen budget is [Unknown].
5. **Competitors.** About 7,000 suppliers, many of them established enrichment companies.
6. **Entry strategy.** Weak. A software-native programme with automated teacher dashboards might stand out [Hypothesis].
7. **Acquisition.** Principals' choice inside a 23k-item catalogue, plus direct school sales. That is effectively direct B2B selling with a compliance gate, and newcomer outcomes are [Unknown].
8. **Operations.** Tender compliance, Hebrew delivery, and likely in-person facilitators. 2025 political turmoil over control of the external-programmes unit (Avi Maoz) [Secondary: snippets].
9–10. Labour-heavy if facilitated. Track A at best.
11. **Kill conditions.** Policy volatility and the size of the catalogue. This also drifts back toward Israel-local, which the brief warns against. **Low.**

## A5. Consumer brand distributed through employee-perks networks (PerkSpot, BenefitHub, Perkbox-type)

1. **Concept.** List a consumer product or subscription as a member discount on employer perk portals.
2. **Customer.** Employees of client companies.
3. **Model.** The merchant funds the discount and/or pays affiliate commissions or placement fees. Terms not public; PerkSpot calls its merchants "advertisers" and pushes "strategic placements".
4. **Evidence (vendor claims).** BenefitHub: "10,000+ Employer Groups", "10 Million Eligible Users", "$1 Billion Annual Global Spend". PerkSpot: "thousands of employers", "millions" [Verified: benefithub.com and perkspot.com merchant pages, 2026-10-13].
5–7. Merchant terms and newcomer outcomes are [Unknown]. My search found no supplier terms. These portals behave like affiliate networks, where large brands with deep discounts dominate [Hypothesis].
8–11. **Verdict:** no evidence that small suppliers earn. It is another catalogue channel, so on Phase 5/6 logic it is not credited. **Weak.**

## A6. Creator revenue share: white-label creator apps and programmes (attempted systematic measurement)

- **Method.** I queried the iTunes Search API for creator-app platform names to see what creator-branded apps achieve.
- **Results (US App Store rating counts):**
  - "Membership+" (Uscreen): 22.
  - Passion.io-built apps under its own seller: Passion.io 293, PassionFighters 185, Badass Body X Fitness 59.
  - BODY by FRIDA: 0.
  - Kajabi Branded App: 3.
  - CoryG Fitness, a creator's own app: 2,677.
  - The platform apps themselves are large (Playbook 36,717; Mighty Networks 74,673) [Verified: itunes.apple.com/search, 2026-10-13].
- **Limits.** This is not a systematic sample. Most white-label apps publish under the creator's own developer account and cannot be found by platform name.
- **Conclusion.** Creator-branded apps mostly gather tens of ratings. The value sits with the creators who already have audiences, which matches Phase 6 (Skool median 11; Ton-piston). I found **no public dataset showing that a product-builder earns meaningful revenue share from creators without being chosen by a big creator**. Kill as a standalone idea. Revenue share stays a tactic: for A1, recruit a foreign-language trainer-influencer as course leader on revenue share.

## Ideas killed (and why)

- **Public-library-licensed consumer e-resources.** The small exemplar (Niche Academy) has pivoted to staff and volunteer training. I found no public newcomer evidence; it is procurement-led and slow.
- **Bank and card-linked offers (Cardlytics-type), telco value-added bundles.** These are merchant-funded and favour large brands, with long partnership sales. No search budget was spent; I found no evidence that small suppliers earn there.
- **Germany DiGA (prescribed apps).** Requires CE medical-device certification and a clinical study, so capital far exceeds budget. Chapter 7 digital prevention is a lighter cousin, but it still needs a registered study.
- **Japan furusato nozei return gifts.** Requires a local Japanese supplier, so not operable from Israel.
- **Student-discount platforms (UNiDAYS/Student Beans).** Just affiliate CPA with no borrowed trust advantage.
- **Israeli perks/meal networks (Cibus, 10bis, Hever).** Restaurant or merchant acceptance networks, not distribution for a new product. Corporate holiday gifting belongs to Agent B.
- **Lifestyle spending accounts (Forma, Benepass).** Employees reimburse almost any eligible merchant, so being "eligible" gives no discovery. Partner pages 404'd and terms are not public.
