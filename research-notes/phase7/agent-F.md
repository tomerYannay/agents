# Phase 7 — Discovery Agent F (contrarian, non-obvious and emerging)

Date: 2026-10-13. Search label: P7-F. WebSearch used: **8 of 8**. Every query is logged in `research-notes/search-log.txt`. Primary pages were opened with WebFetch, curl or public APIs (SEC EDGAR, legislation.gov.uk, the official MCP Registry API).

## Headline (read first)

I tested the four assigned areas for public evidence that a newcomer can **borrow, contract or inherit** a customer relationship.

1. **Commission-agency and residual models are the strongest finding, but not as the classic manufacturer's rep.**
   - The commission flows that are well documented, publicly valued and actively traded are **recurring residuals in US technology-services (telecom/cloud) agency and in merchant-services (card processing) agency.**
   - The best evidence is ScanSource's FY2026 10-K: Intelisys net billings of about **$2.88B, up only 3.2%**. The channel is mature and slow-growing.
   - Residual books trade at disclosed multiples, and Avant now advances commissions to advisors (Oct 2026).
   - **No source gives newcomer outcomes for new advisors or agents.**
2. **Classic manufacturer's-rep (MANA) data is stale.**
   - MANA's own commission survey stopped in **2005** because participation fell "below an acceptable level".
   - MANA says rep-firm valuation knowledge is scarce, and buyers struggle to get conventional loans.
   - There is no public evidence base, and the model is relationship- and travel-heavy. I rank it low.
3. **Reverse licensing has real but tiny precedent.**
   - One verified small-operator deal: a **$1 fee, 5%-then-2.5% royalty licence of a paused Amazon health brand** (Restart Life Sciences, March 2025). It is still pre-launch.
   - Brand-revival operators who succeed (Ultimate Products with Beldray and Kleeneze) already own retail distribution.
4. **AI-agent-mediated buying (agentic commerce) is a negative finding for 2026.**
   - OpenAI moved Instant Checkout out of chat into Apps in **March 2026**.
   - Forrester counted about **30 Shopify merchants** by Feb 2026.
   - A secondary report says Walmart's ChatGPT checkout converted at about **one-third** of its own site.
   - The MCP-server channel shows the same supply flood as Phase 5's marketplaces: fewer than 5% of servers monetised (blog figures), and the official registry is growing fast (see F6).
   - **Being a supplier into AI agents does not yet qualify as a borrowed-distribution channel with newcomer evidence.**

None of the six candidates below has evidence that a newcomer acquires customers. The two most useful leads both concern a **liquid, priced asset class (residual books)**. Owning that asset could let the founder buy distribution rather than build it, but the evidence is mostly vendor- or broker-sourced.

## F1. Vertical "technology advisor" practice under a TSD, with sub-agent referral partners

1. **Concept.** Become a commission agent (technology advisor, TA) under a US technology services distributor such as Telarus, Avant or Intelisys.
   - Sell connectivity, UCaaS, contact centre, cybersecurity and cloud to one vertical (e.g., multi-site dental, logistics yards, franchise systems).
   - Earn monthly residuals for the life of each contract.
   - Recruit **sub-agents** (MSPs, IT consultants, vertical accountants) who refer deals for a share of the residual.
2. **Paying customer.**
   - The suppliers (carriers and SaaS vendors) pay commissions through the TSD.
   - The end customers are US SMB and mid-market multi-site businesses.
3. **Model.**
   - Commissions are "typically 12–22% of the monthly invoice", paid monthly for the customer lifetime [Secondary: Channel Futures snippet; page returned 403, unverified].
   - TSDs charge advisors no upfront fees [Verified: telarus.com/partners, accessed 2026-10-13].
4. **Spending evidence.**
   - Intelisys (ScanSource) FY2026 net billings were about **$2.88B, +3.2% y/y**. Segment net sales were $101.1M (+3.1%) at a 98.6% gross margin, and segment operating income was $28.6M [Verified: ScanSource 10-K FY2026, sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/918965/000091896526000044/scsc-20260630.htm, filed 2026-08-20].
   - The 10-K says advisors "earn monthly commissions on contracted sales orders… based on a recurring revenue model", and Intelisys has contracts with 200+ carriers and cloud providers [same].
   - Telarus reported 4,000 selling advisors and about 300 suppliers [Verified: telarus.com/?p=5604, dated 2022-02-10].
   - Avant launched a standardised programme in Oct 2026 that advances first-term commissions to advisors in exchange for a higher split and exclusivity [Verified: channeldive.com, 2026-10-08]. Distributors are financing advisors, which shows that **advisor residual streams are bankable**.
5. **Competitors and dislikes.**
   - Thousands of incumbent advisors already compete, plus the suppliers' direct sales teams.
   - ScanSource names Avant and Telarus as its TSD competitors.
   - Customer dislikes are [Unknown]. The channel trade press reports channel conflict (Channel Futures headline, unverified).
6. **Entry strategy.** Pick one vertical with multi-site, recurring connectivity and security needs. Use the founder's automation to:
   - build vertical prospect lists (permits, licence registries, franchise disclosure documents);
   - run AI-assisted outbound;
   - sign 5–10 vertical-adjacent sub-agents (vertical-software resellers, MSPs) who already hold the customer relationship.

   The founder's edge is the tooling, not the product. The differentiation is modest; I am not claiming a structural edge.
7. **Acquisition and newcomer evidence.**
   - **[Unknown]: no public data on first-year earnings or survival of new advisors** (the search found none; Channel Futures returned 403).
   - The TSDs' "apply in 5 minutes, no upfront costs" pitch suggests large numbers of low-activity advisors [Hypothesis].
   - Sub-agent recruitment is itself a sales task.
8. **Capital, operations and operability.**
   - Near-zero capital. The work is quoting, ordering and escalation support, much of it handled by the TSD's engineers ("quote-to-cash support" [Verified: Telarus]).
   - **Israel operability is [Unknown].** Partner pages state no US-entity or W-9 requirement, but none says foreign advisors are accepted. A US LLC is probably needed [Hypothesis].
   - The time zone allows US-morning calls from Israel afternoons.
9. **Labour dependence.** Early revenue depends entirely on the founder's selling. Residuals then compound without new founder hours, and sub-agents add revenue without founder hours. This is one of the few models where revenue can **decouple from hours** once the book exists.
10. **Unit economics (all [Hypothesis]).**
    - One $3,000/month multi-site connectivity, voice and security deal at 15% earns about $450/month for 36+ months.
    - Track A ($3k/month) needs about 7 such deals net of churn.
    - Track B ($15k/month) needs about 35 deals, or a sub-agent network.
    - Sub-agent splits erode margin (splits are [Unknown]).
    - Sales cycles of 3–9 months are typical for telecom [Hypothesis]. Speed to first cash is slow.
11. **Risks and killers.**
    - Mature, slow-growth channel (+3.2% billings).
    - Suppliers can claw back or cut commissions.
    - A remote founder with no US references is the same "newcomer gets nothing" pattern measured on 11 channels.
    - Foreign advisor eligibility.

## F2. Buying small residual books (merchant-services or telecom agency), then layering referral partners

1. **Concept.** Do not originate. **Buy existing residual streams** from retiring or exiting small agents, then grow them by cross-selling to the inherited merchants and by adding referral partners. The customer relationship is literally inherited.
2. **Paying customer.**
   - Processors or ISOs pay the residuals.
   - The underlying customers are US SMB merchants.
3. **Model.** Pay a lump sum (often with an attrition earn-out) and receive monthly residuals.
4. **Spending and transaction evidence.**
   - The 10-K filings below are **verified as search-result titles only**; I did not open the filings.
     - Deluxe's FY2025 10-K mentions a "residual commission buy-out agreement" in Q4 2025.
     - Shift4's 10-K describes residual buyouts as a lump sum plus contingent payments tied to attrition.
   - Nuvei runs a "Portfolio Buyouts for ISOs" page [search result].
   - Broker benchmarks [Anecdote/vendor: ctacquisitions.com, verified June 24, 2026]:
     - 3–6× monthly residuals for "fast-turn" high-churn books (above 16% annual attrition);
     - 18–30× for premium vertical books;
     - ISV-embedded books carry 3–6% annual attrition, against 12–15% for commodity retail.
   - Older reference points: a 2020 NAB offer of 36× on 50% of residuals [Secondary: Greensheet], and "upper 40s" multiples cited in **2016** [Verified: digitaltransactions.net, article dated 2016-05-04; stale].
5. **Competitors.** Processors themselves (Nuvei, Shift4, Deluxe) and consolidators are the buyers. Small books are their crumbs.
6. **Entry strategy.** Buy only books **below the consolidators' threshold** (the broker's own focus is $1M–$25M annual run-rate). That means $200–$2,000/month books, in verticals with low attrition. Then:
   - re-engage the merchants with software extras (online ordering, review tools) to cut attrition;
   - add referral partners such as local POS installers.

   Whether processors allow transfer of tiny books is the crux [Unknown].
7. **Acquisition and newcomer evidence.** Deal flow would come from agent forums, broker lists and processors' own agent bases [Hypothesis]. **There are no public data on deal volume for sub-$50k books.**
8. **Capital and operability.**
   - At 3–6× monthly residuals, the $2,000 budget buys only about $330–$670/month of high-churn residuals. Premium books cost 18–30×.
   - **Flag: anything beyond a micro test exceeds the budget.**
   - Merchant-services agent agreements typically require a US entity [Hypothesis].
   - Residuals are assignable only with ISO consent [Hypothesis].
9. **Labour.** Very low once bought. Servicing is the processor's job.
10. **Economics.** A 3–6× multiple on a book losing at least 16% a year implies a fast nominal payback, but the yield depends wholly on attrition and on the processor honouring the transfer [Hypothesis]. Track A needs about $50–100k of purchases (at 18–30×, a $3k/month book). That is a capital play, not a $2k test.
11. **Risks and killers.**
    - Processor repricing or clawbacks.
    - Non-transferability.
    - Fraud in residual statements.
    - A capital requirement that rises 25–50× for good books.
    - This is **close in spirit to ledger L13 and P6 "buying offline businesses with seller financing"**. It is materially different because the asset is a contractual residual stream with a liquid buyer market, but it shares the capital problem.

## F3. Reverse-licensing paused or dormant consumer brands (royalty licence instead of purchase)

1. **Concept.** License the trademark, listings and formulation of a **paused but formerly selling** e-commerce brand. Pay a nominal fee and a single-digit royalty with a purchase option, then relaunch. The inherited asset is the brand's search and review history and its supplier set.
2. **Paying customer.** Consumers on Amazon and other marketplaces, in the US or UK.
3. **Model.** Product margin minus a 2.5–5% royalty.
4. **Deal evidence.**
   - Restart Life Sciences (CSE: HEAL) licensed a paused Amazon health-care line on these terms [Verified: newsfilecorp release 297680, 2026-05-15]:
     - $1 fee, exclusive 5-year term plus a 5-year renewal;
     - 5% royalty on gross sales up to $1M, then 2.5%;
     - an option to buy the trademarks.

     **It was still not launched 14 months after signing.** No sales or review data were disclosed.
   - Ultimate Products (UK) buys dead brands out of administration and relaunches them: Kleeneze in 2018, and Beldray, bought in 2009 and called its "best-performing brand" [Verified: housewareslive.net, 2018-06-14]. Ultimate Products already sells to UK retailers. **The distribution was theirs, not the brand's.**
5. **Competitors.** Amazon aggregators (Thrasio and others) and brand-acquisition funds. Their post-2023 retreat is from memory [Secondary] and should be verified.
6. **Entry strategy.** Target brands too small for aggregators (under $100k past annual sales) whose owners stopped trading, and offer a royalty instead of cash. **Whether paused Amazon listings keep enough review and rank value to matter is [Unknown].**
7. **Acquisition and newcomer evidence.** None measurable. The single verified deal had no outcome 14 months in.
8. **Capital and operations.**
   - Inventory, fees and ads will exceed $2,000 per relaunch [Hypothesis; flag].
   - FBA from Israel is workable but needs a US or UK entity.
   - Brand Registry authorisation must come from the rights owner.
9. **Labour.** Moderate. Ops can be delegated through a 3PL and a VA.
10. **Economics.** [Unknown]. Typical private-label net margins apply, minus 5%. Track A is plausible per brand only if the listing retains demand.
11. **Risks.**
    - Revived listings may have lost rank.
    - Owners may already have sold the inventory.
    - This drifts toward "low-capital physical products", a category earlier phases leaned on.
    - No newcomer outcome data.

## F4. UK/EU commercial agent for non-EU (including Israeli) goods manufacturers, protected by statutory indemnity

1. **Concept.** Act as a commission agent for manufacturers outside Europe (Israeli industrial, agri and medical consumables makers, or US makers) selling **goods** B2B into the UK or EU. Hold territory contracts and delegate to sub-agents.
   - This is **materially different from killed L5** (first Israeli distributor of EU brands). L5 meant inventory and import into Israel. F4 has no inventory, runs outbound from Israel, and the agent carries a statutory asset.
2. **Paying customer.** The manufacturers (principals) pay; the buyers are UK/EU distributors and OEMs.
3. **Model.**
   - Commission on orders. MANA's last published ranges were 5–7% OEM, 5–9% distributor and 7–14% end-user [Secondary: MANA survey via search snippet]. **These date from 2005 or earlier; MANA says no survey since 2005** [Verified: members.manaonline.org/commission-survey, accessed 2026-10-13].
   - **Contrarian asset:** under UK Commercial Agents Regulations 1993 reg. 17, a terminated agent who brought customers gets an indemnity of up to **one year's average annual remuneration over the preceding five years**, or compensation [Verified: legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1993/3053/regulation/17, accessed 2026-10-13]. The EU Directive 86/653 equivalent could not be fetched (EUR-Lex returned an empty page).
   - The regulations cover **goods only**, so they do not protect software or services agencies.
4. **Spending evidence.**
   - MANA's Nov 2024 associate-member survey found 84% of manufacturers have written rep contracts and about half have used reps for 10+ years. Only about 200 of 1,500 associates responded, and MANA says it is "not statistically accurate" [Verified: members.manaonline.org news, 2024-11-25].
   - **No evidence of Israeli manufacturers seeking UK/EU agents.** The P6 search found mostly the reverse direction.
5. **Competitors.** Established multi-line agencies and the principals' own export managers.
6. **Entry strategy.** "None credible" beyond picking a niche. The founder has no industry relationships, and the model relies on them.
7. **Channel evidence.** RepHunter, MANA RepFinder and LineFinder exist, but the directories are members-only. RepHunter shows no listing counts publicly [Verified: rephunter.net, accessed 2026-10-13].
8. **Operability.** A remote agent from Israel is plausible for spec-driven B2B. Trade shows cost money.
9. **Labour.** High: selling is the founder's own labour until sub-agents exist.
10. **Economics.** [Hypothesis] Track A needs about €400–700k a year of principal sales at 6–9% commission. That is a multi-year build.
11. **Killer.** No relationships and no newcomer evidence. Principals typically terminate reps once a territory grows, though the indemnity softens this in the UK and EU.

## F5. Payments-residual agent to small vertical-software vendors (ISV payments enablement)

1. **Concept.** Many small vertical SaaS vendors (salon, tutoring, field-service, club software) still do not monetise their customers' card payments. The founder:
   - builds the processor integration (founder skill);
   - signs the ISV to an ISO or processor partner programme;
   - takes an agent share of the residual, with the ISV keeping its own share.

   This is borrowed distribution: the ISV owns the merchants.
2. **Paying customer.** The processor or ISO pays residuals. The ISVs are in the US, UK and Israel.
3. **Model.** A residual split (basis points on volume). Splits were not found [Unknown].
4. **Evidence.**
   - ISV-embedded books have the **lowest attrition (3–6% a year)** and the highest valuations (14–22× EBITDA) in the broker's table [Vendor: ctacquisitions.com, 2026-06-24].
   - ScanSource's 10-K lists ISOs that sell card processing as a channel-partner class [Verified].
5. **Competitors.**
   - Payfac-as-a-service vendors (Finix, Infinicept, Payroc and others [from memory; Unknown counts]).
   - Stripe Connect's self-serve route.
   - Processors' own ISV teams.

   The wedge is ISVs too small for processors' ISV desks.
6. **Entry strategy.** Pick one software niche where vendors publicly lack integrated payments: check their pricing pages for "we integrate with Square/Stripe" versus native payments. That scan can be done by desk research later.
7. **Acquisition and newcomer evidence.** [Unknown]. ISVs are reachable (public lists of vertical SaaS), but **no newcomer outcome data**.
8. **Operability.** Integration work is code, which risks "selling dev hours" unless the founder is paid only in residuals. ISO agent status likely needs a US entity [Hypothesis].
9. **Labour.** Upfront integration per ISV, then passive residuals.
10. **Economics.** [Hypothesis] An ISV with 300 merchants × $15k/month volume × 0.25% split earns the founder about $11k/month at full adoption. Real adoption is often far below 100%.
11. **Risks.**
    - ISVs prefer Stripe Connect and keep the whole margin.
    - The processor relationship is controlled by others.
    - The founder ends up as an unpaid integrator.

## F6. Supplier into AI agents (MCP / ChatGPT Apps data or product provider)

1. **Concept.** Supply a niche dataset or transactional capability (inventory, availability, pricing) as an MCP server or ChatGPT App, and charge per call or by subscription.
2. **Evidence.**
   - **Agentic checkout retreat:**
     - OpenAI scaled back integrated commerce in March 2026; "Instant Checkout is moving to Apps" [Verified: shopifreaks.com, 2026-03-09, citing The Information];
     - about 30 Shopify merchants by Feb 2026 [Secondary: Forrester via search snippet];
     - Walmart's ChatGPT checkout converted at about one-third of its own site [Secondary, unverified];
     - in Feb 2026, OpenAI had not yet set up sales-tax collection [Verified: same Shopifreaks article].
   - **MCP monetisation:**
     - fewer than 5% of 11–17k servers are monetised;
     - 21st.dev's "$10k MRR in 6 weeks" is repeated without a primary source;
     - MCPize hosts 950+ servers from 450+ developers at an 80% revenue share;
     - one developer reported 0 paying users after 2 weeks.

     All of these are [Anecdote/blogs].
   - **Official MCP Registry** [Verified: registry.modelcontextprotocol.io/v0/servers API, paged on 2026-10-13]:
     - My crawl hit its 400-page cap at **13,953 unique server names**, so the true total is higher. 11,619 of those advertise remote endpoints.
     - Publish dates of the first entry seen per name, by month:

       | Month | Servers |
       |---|---|
       | Jan 2026 | 56 |
       | Apr 2026 | 635 |
       | Jul 2026 | 1,870 |
       | Sep 2026 | **3,911** |
       | Oct 1–13, 2026 | **2,431** |

     - Caveat: a date may reflect a later version rather than the first publish.
     - Supply is roughly doubling every two months, which is the Apify/CodeCanyon flood pattern again.
3. **Verdict.** This is the Phase 5 marketplace pattern in a new costume: a supply flood with no measured newcomer purchases. Agent-mediated buying exists, but the purchase moved back to merchants' apps and sites, so **there is no independent supplier channel to borrow.** The excluded "agentic-commerce/GEO services" idea is the only monetisation, and it is out of scope. **Rank low; watch only.**

## Killed or not pursued

- **Classic US manufacturer's rep (new firm, or buying a retiring rep firm).**
  - No commission data since 2005 (MANA).
  - Rep-firm valuation is opaque and buyers lack bank financing [Verified search finding: MANA March 2025 session; Tiburon Aug 2025, terms undisclosed].
  - Principals can usually terminate on short notice in the US, where there is no statutory indemnity.
  - Buying one overlaps the P6 "buy offline business with seller financing" exclusion.
- **Supplier to ChatGPT Instant Checkout / Agentic Commerce Protocol.** The channel was scaled back in March 2026, with about 30 merchants participating (above).
- **Country-master licence or reseller of foreign SaaS.** Overlaps ledger L5, L11 and "generic reseller programmes".
- **Art/character licensing agency.** Not researched (search budget). It is heavily relationship- and trade-show-driven, which suits this founder poorly.
- **Dormant-brand outright purchase.** Capital-heavy. Restart's licensing structure (F3) is the only low-capital variant found.
