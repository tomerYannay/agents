# Phase 2: Expanded Opportunity Search

**Date:** 2026-10-12
**Companion to:** `business-opportunity-report-2026-10-12.md` (Phase 1)

## How the research was done

- Five independent discovery agents (A–E) each covered a different universe and could not see each other's work.
- After that, an Evidence Auditor and a Red-Team Investor reviewed the top 10. They also worked independently of each other.
- I (the coordinator) did the screening, scoring and synthesis.

## Important limits

- **The web-search quota ran out early.** The session hit its 200-search cap during discovery. Most agents completed only about 15 searches. After that they could only fetch specific URLs directly.
- **Several sites refused automated fetching.** Etsy, Amazon, kolzchut, the gov.uk agent pages and others returned 403 or 404 errors.
- **Many discovery claims rest on search snippets or vendor blogs.** The auditor re-checked the claims the rankings depend on by fetching the pages directly. Claims about absence ("no competitor exists") mostly **could not be verified**.
- **No customers were contacted.** Every "would buy" claim remains a hypothesis.

---

## Bottom line

**Phase 2 did not find an opportunity that is clearly stronger than the Phase 1 winners.**
- The best new candidate is **UK Making Tax Digital (MTD) white-label prep for accounting practices (T8)**. Its evidence-adjusted score is 56, against 51.5–54 for Phase 1's winners.
- That 2–4 point gap is inside the margin of judgment, so the scores alone don't settle it.

**What did improve:**
1. **Larger markets reachable with fewer of the founder's own hours.**
   - T8's mandated population grows to about 3M UK taxpayers by 2028 (the 2028 total is a secondary figure I didn't re-verify).
   - The demand is recurring every quarter.
   - It doesn't require selling the founder's hours, provided automation works.
2. **A pattern that survived both research rounds.** The pattern is AI-prepared back-office work done upstream of accountants' existing software, triggered by digital-tax mandates.
   - It came up in Israel in Phase 1 (allocation numbers, P3) and in the UK in Phase 2 (MTD).
   - In both rounds, independent agents converged on it.
   - In both rounds, audit found competitors already in the market.
   - The demand is real. A differentiated way in is not yet proven.
3. **One regulation is confirmed binding with no small-business relief:** the EU packaging regulation's (PPWR) authorised-representative (AR) requirement for non-EU sellers (T1).
   - The auditor confirmed Art. 45(3) from the EUR-Lex primary text.
   - The 2035 deferral never applied to non-EU producers.
   - However, the software layer for it was copied within weeks of the deadline.

**What did not improve:** buyer-specific evidence. Most candidates died on competitor checks, as in Phase 1:
- WeeklySafety already sells English/Spanish toolbox talks at $25/month.
- BlueCargo already sells risk-free demurrage-and-detention (D&D) audits.
- Two Shopify PPWR apps launched in Jul–Sep 2026.
- Schooley Mitchell runs 350+ utility-audit offices.

## 6. The three opportunities most worth testing with real customers

### 1. T8: AI prep of quarterly updates, done upstream of the accountant's software, for UK practices with many landlord clients
- **Offer:** The practice forwards client records (letting-agent statements, bank exports, receipts). We return categorised quarterly totals mapped to MTD categories, plus an exceptions list, within 24 hours. The practice files through its own recognised software and Agent Services Account.
- **Test price:** £15–20 per client per quarter. Pilot: 10 clients free.
- **Why test it:** A mandated, growing, quarterly workload (£30k threshold from 6 Apr 2027). Accountants are a concentrated B2B channel. The Israel–UK time zones work, and Israel has UK data adequacy (supported inference).
- **Economics (hypothesis):**
  - At £17.50/quarter, revenue is about £5.80 per client per month.
  - At 70% gross margin, about £4 per client per month is left.
  - Track A at roughly £2.4k/month profit needs about 600 active clients, for example 15 practices × 40 clients.
  - Track B needs about 3,000 or more clients.
  - That only works if at least 80% of documents are processed with no human touch. Otherwise it becomes a bookkeeping agency.
- **Largest unproven assumption:** practices will pay at least £15 per client per quarter to a foreign AI-first provider, when offshore bookkeepers and client-side apps already exist.

### 2. T1: "EU packaging handled — one fee, all countries" for UK DTC brands (service layer, not an app)
- **Offer:**
  - A €490 compliance map from the brand's order export: which countries, what volumes, which obligations.
  - Then €150–250/month to coordinate registrations, licences and ARs through partners such as Lizenzero, plus declarations and deadlines.
- **Why test it:** Confirmed binding obligation, no relief for non-EU sellers, Amazon.de blocks unregistered listings, and paying non-EU customers already exist (Lizenzero reviews).
- **Economics (hypothesis):**
  - 30 clients × €200/month ≈ €6k/month revenue.
  - Licence and AR fees pass through to the client.
  - Track B needs about 150 or more clients, or a referral margin on licences.
- **Largest unproven assumption:** brands will pay a coordinator on top of Lizenzero's all-in €299 per country. The red-team also notes some merchants are simply stopping EU shipping.
- **Israel note:** possible reluctance from EU partners to work with an Israeli company. Not evidenced.

### 3. T2: FileCabinet CS bulk extractor (a time-boxed software sprint)
- **Offer:** Self-serve local tool that exports a firm's FileCabinet CS archive to foldered, tagged PDFs. $990 for up to 300 clients, $1,990 for up to 1,500.
- **Why test it:** It is pure software, so no founder hours per sale. Competitors' price anchors are confirmed. The test is fast: a parse-feasibility check plus a waitlist backed by deposits.
- **Economics (hypothesis):**
  - 4 sales/month × about $1,500 ≈ $6k/month, with near-zero cost of goods, until about end-2027.
  - **Track A only. No Track B.**
- **Largest unproven assumptions:** the format can be parsed reliably, and firms not moving to Onvio will trust a foreign vendor's local software with taxpayer files.

**Not chosen, with reasons:**
- **T6:** a me-too product.
- **T3:** an incumbent already offers risk-free audits.
- **T10:** a narrow annual window with unknown savings size. Still a sensible local fallback if you have warm access to retail chains.
- **P1:** still valid as a fallback for cash flow, but it is selling your own hours.

## Research limitations (Phase 2)

- **The session's web-search cap (200) was hit during discovery.** Competitor-absence checks, Acquire/Flippa comparables, G2 reviews, Upwork spend data and most Hebrew searches were largely impossible after that. Several "no competitor found" statements are therefore **unknown**, not negative. The red-team noted the quota refills over time, so a targeted rerun on T8 and T1 is possible.
- **Unverified items:**
  - the Dutch €2.4–4.3k cost figure (ppc.land);
  - the Etsy shop sales counts and Etsy payouts to Israel;
  - UK white-label MTD pricing;
  - the size of HMRC's first wave;
  - the AICPA contingency rules;
  - the Israeli arnona consultant fee norms and non-lawyer filing;
  - a TR primary source for the FileCabinet CS end-of-life date.
- **One competitor price anchor was misattributed:** "eenovators $800/building" is a Kenyan firm. This is a reminder that snippet-based evidence needs checking.
- **Scores are structured judgments.** Differences under about 5 points are not meaningful.
