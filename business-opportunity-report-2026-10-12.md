## Deliverable 1 — Executive Summary

**The main finding is negative.** None of the 22 candidates came out of audit and red-team with Medium-or-better evidence that customers would buy *our* offer. Every finalist lost ground on review, and three of six claims of "no competitor found" were contradicted. Evidence-adjusted balanced scores fall in a narrow 42–54 band. The top three are within 2.5 points of each other, which is inside the margin of judgment, so **the scores cannot pick the winner. Cheap behavioral tests have to.**

Patterns that held up:
1. **Regulation-forced Israeli back-office work is real, but fast local startups are already serving it.**
   - Example: the חשבוניות ישראל allocation-number threshold. It dropped to ₪5,000 on 1.6.2026 (verified). Non-allocated invoices also lose the income-tax deduction for expenses paid from 1.8.2025 (strong secondary source).
   - The opportunity is real. But Luca AI and Finbot already sell to the same buyer at the price we would have proposed.
   - Lesson: in Israel, an obvious regulatory gap gets filled within months.
2. **Platform deadlines create paid technical work an Israeli developer can sell internationally without a local sales presence.**
   - Atlassian Connect end-of-support is 31 Jan 2027. Data Center end-of-life is 28 Mar 2029. Both are verified.
   - This fits the founder's skills best. It is closer to specialised contracting than to a company.
3. **Marketplace apps (Atlassian Forge, monday) are the only scalable route found where an Israeli founder avoids enterprise sales.** But per-niche demand evidence is thin, and outcomes are heavily skewed.

**Recommendation:** spend the first week testing **Opportunity F4-S.** It means selling Forge/Connect migration and rebuild capacity to Atlassian Solution Partners, white-label, plus fixed-price "customization triage" sold directly to mid-size Data Center customers.
- It has the strongest *verified* urgency of any candidate.
- It is the best fit with the founder's skills.
- It can be tested for close to $0.
- It is primarily a cash-flow play. Its route to a scalable business (repeated gaps turned into Forge apps) is a **hypothesis** with weak evidence so far.

## Deliverable 4 — Finalists (post-audit facts)

### F1 — Allocation-number guard for bookkeeping offices
- **Verified:**
  - Threshold history: ₪10k from 1.1.2026, then ₪5k from 1.6.2026. Sources: Bizportal 19.5.2026 https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/20032314; Capitax 24.5.2026 https://www.capitax.co.il/content/2/3593.
  - The "₪5k in 2028" claim came from the original schedule, which was brought forward in March 2025 (ice.co.il 17.3.2025).
  - An invoice without an allocation number cannot be used to claim input VAT.
  - From 1.8.2025 it is also not a deductible income-tax expense, under Income Tax Ordinance s.32(18). Secondary source: Efraty CPA 22.2.2026 https://www.efraty.com/?p=5441.
- **Contradicts the opportunity:**
  - **Luca AI** (https://luca-ai.io/) does WhatsApp/email intake and AI extraction for bookkeeping offices. Its "Compliance Shield" flags a missing or badly formatted allocation number, and it exports to Hashavshevet and Priority. Price: **₪490/mo for 40 clients, ₪990/mo for 150, ₪2,490/mo for 500.**
  - **Finbot** has marketed automated allocation-number checks to CPAs since 5/2024.
  - Hashavshevet has per-invoice verification built in.
  - Priority does batch verification.
  - Procee pulls received-invoice details through the Tax Authority API.
  - The PCN874 simulator reportedly cross-checks invoices at filing time (single source).
- **Remaining gap:** Luca says it checks for a missing or badly formatted number. It does not claim to verify the number live against Tax Authority records. Live verification is possible: the ITA developer portal plus a client OAuth token that must be renewed about every 3 months; Procee shows it works. Supplier chasing is the other gap.
- **Unknown:** whether bookkeepers feel this pain enough to pay, since no complaints were found; and how many offices exist.
- **Effect on the economics:** the market already prices this at about ₪12 per bookkept client per month (Luca's Starter tier). The ₪490-per-client pilot idea does not survive.

### F2 — Customs-broker document extraction
- **Verified:** salaried data-entry postings for "Shaar Olami" and "מסלול".
- **Contradicted:** "no Israeli AI competitor". AiDock (Rehovot, about $3.5M raised) offers extraction and HS classification for customs declarants. CargoSeer is also active.
- **Unknown:** who the broker-software vendors are, their import formats, and how many brokers are licensed. The Comptroller (2016) counted about 550 bodies of all types connecting to Shaar Olami.

### F3 — Insurance commission reconciliation
- **Contradicted:** commission.co.il auto-detects report formats from 7+ insurers, offers agency tiers, and claims 500+ active agents. Insurecheck has run a commission-audit service since 2002.
- **Verified context:** ₪13.23B in agent commissions (2025 Capital Market Authority report, via funder.co.il).
- **Status:** eliminated. No uncovered segment was identified.

### F4 — Atlassian DC→Cloud rebuilds → Forge apps
- **Verified:**
  - Data Center end of sale to new customers: 30.3.2026.
  - Expansions until 30.3.2028. End of life: 28.3.2029 (https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/data-center-end-of-life).
  - **Connect end-of-support: 31.1.2027.**
  - More than 95% of paid app seats are already on Forge.
  - Forge revenue share is 0% up to $1M lifetime, per partner, Forge-only, then 16–17%.
  - Confluence user macros have no Cloud equivalent.
- **Weakening:**
  - Atlassian directs buyers to Cloud Specialized Partners, whose services include custom apps.
  - The Forge MCP server (GA 3/2026) lets in-house developers rebuild simple macros with AI.
  - Forge macros cannot change content outside their own body, so some customizations cannot be faithfully rebuilt.
  - Marketplace demand in this niche is thin: the generic user-macro app has about 50 installs, and Macro Studio (9/2026) has 1.
  - Adaptavist sells an AI ScriptRunner migration suite.
- **Unknown (the crux):** whether partners subcontract, at what rates, and whether mid-size Data Center customers buy small fixed-price triage.

### F5 — monday.com app portfolio
- **Verified:** 850+ apps, 1.6M installs, and 49% of accounts have at least one app installed. Revenue share is 0% to $200k lifetime, then 85/15. Pioneera's $30k MRR comes from monday's own blog (self-reported, portfolio of apps).
- **Weakening:** "monday vibe" is a native AI app builder available to all paid accounts. TimelinesAI covers WhatsApp. iCount connects through Make.
- **Possible wedge:** no native Morning/Green Invoice or iCount monday app was found. Search found none, so this is Unknown, not confirmed as absent.

### F6 — Amendment 13 kit
- **Verified:** in force 14.8.2025. The Privacy Protection Authority (PPA) began sector inspections in 12/2025 that include צהרונים and online retail. The first fine was ₪256k against **Meuhedet**, not Maccabi, on 21.7.2026, for reporting a breach late.
- **The two reviewers disagree:**
  - The auditor raises it: no packaged SMB offer was found, and prices are scattered from ₪380 to ₪5,000+.
  - The red-team calls it nearly dead:
    - Enforcement so far hit a large health fund.
    - The sector inspections are questionnaire-based.
    - צהרונים operators are cash-strapped; the Ministry cut ₪250M from Nitzanim.
    - The Standards Institute sells an Amendment 13 gap audit with its own approval stamp.
    - A solo developer selling compliance documents risks giving unauthorised legal advice.
- **My resolution:** the red-team's points are about who will *pay*. The auditor's points are about the *problem*. Willingness to pay is what decides this, so I lean toward the red-team.

**Day 3 — Prepare materials.** Outreach script (below), discovery questions, a rate card, and a short sample statement of work.

**Day 5 — Hold calls.**

**Day 7 — Decide go, pivot or stop** against the criteria below.

**Outreach script (partner version):**
> Hi [Name] — I'm a backend/Forge developer focused on one thing: rebuilding Connect apps and Data Center customizations (user macros, ScriptRunner scripts, custom plugins) on Forge ahead of the Jan 31, 2027 Connect cutoff and the 2029 DC end-of-life. I work white-label under your brand, start within a week, and overlap EU hours. Here's a 90-sec demo of a DC user macro rebuilt as a Forge macro: [link]. Do you currently have migration projects where custom-app rebuilds are the bottleneck? If so, I'd offer a paid 2-hour scoping on one of them so you can judge the work before committing more.

**Five discovery questions (about past behaviour):**
1. In your last 3 Data Center→Cloud migrations, what happened to the custom apps, macros and scripts? Who rebuilt them, and how long did it take?
2. Have you subcontracted any Atlassian development in the past 12 months? To whom, at what rate, and what went wrong?
3. Have you turned down, delayed, or descoped a project in the past 6 months because of developer capacity?
4. How many of your current clients still have Connect apps or Data Center customizations that aren't solved yet?
5. When you last brought in an outside developer, what did they have to show or sign before you'd let them touch client work?

**Measurable validation criteria (within 7 days, extended to 14 for payment):**
- At least 30 qualified outreaches sent.
- **Reply rate of 15% or more** (at least 5 replies).
- **3 or more discovery calls.**
- **At least 2 prospects confirm** they have subcontracted or turned down Atlassian development work in the past 12 months.
- **At least 1 paid commitment:** a paid scoping call, a $1,500 triage, or a signed capacity block at $50/hr or more.

**Reject (stop) if any of these happens:**
- Fewer than 2 interested replies from 30 messages.
- Zero partners have subcontracted in the past year.
- Stated acceptable rates are below about $40/hr.
- Prospects consistently say in-house AI tooling or Atlassian's own migration tools already cover it.
- No paid commitment by day 14.

**Pivot signals:**
- **Interest is strong but only for ScriptRunner work:** narrow the offer to that.
- **Direct Data Center customers respond better than partners:** switch to the direct triage offer.
- **Several prospects name the same unsolved gap:** that gap becomes the Forge app candidate.

## Deliverable 9 — Research Limitations

- **No customer evidence at all.** Every "would buy" claim is a hypothesis. The scores rank only how good the opportunities look *on paper*.
- **Competitor traction is self-reported:** Luca AI pricing, commission.co.il's "500+ agents", Pioneera's $30k MRR. None was independently verified.
- **Missing market counts:** bookkeeping/CPA offices in Israel, licensed customs brokers, Israeli monday accounts, Atlassian partners willing to subcontract.
- **No rate data** for Atlassian Forge subcontracting. The F4-S economics rest on an assumed range.
- **Single-source items:**
  - The PCN874 simulator cross-checking allocation numbers.
  - Carrefour's court-ordered app accessibility (relevant to S7).
- **Unverified:**
  - Whether Rivhit, iCount, SUMIT or Morning verify allocation numbers on *received* invoices.
  - Whether ITA API access requires software-house registration.
- **Inaccessible sources:** Facebook groups (where Israeli bookkeepers and SMBs discuss these issues); some gov.il pages returned 403.
- **Assumptions that could change the recommendation:**
  - **Warm network:** if you have warm access to bookkeepers, insurance agents or customs brokers, the Israel-local options become faster to test, and F1 may overtake F4-S.
  - **Availability:** if you can work close to full-time for 3 months, F4-S becomes more viable, both its capacity limits and its upside.
  - **Preference for local work:** if you strongly prefer Hebrew-market, local work, F1 with a narrow wedge (live verification plus supplier chasing) is the default.
  - **Risk appetite:** if you would accept a 12-month runway with no revenue for a Track B bet, a marketplace-app portfolio could be re-examined. But this research found no evidenced niche for one.
- Scores are structured judgments, not measured probabilities of success.
