# Phase 3: Unexplored Opportunity Discovery

**Date:** 2026-10-12
**Companion reports:** `business-opportunity-report-2026-10-12.md` (Round 1), `business-opportunity-report-phase2-2026-10-12.md` (Round 2)

## How this round was run

- **Six independent discovery agents (A–F).** Each worked a different market universe and could not see the others' work. Each had a cap of 14 web searches; together they used 81.
- **Shortlist.** I picked 14 candidates from their findings.
- **Two independent reviews of the shortlist:**
  - An Evidence Auditor, who used 6 searches before the session-wide search quota ran out and then fetched pages directly.
  - A Red-Team Investor, who used 13 searches before the quota ran out.
- **Scoring and comparison.** I rescored the 14 and the 5 strongest earlier-round ideas with one rubric (section 6).

No customers were contacted, nothing was bought, and nothing was built.

---

## Bottom line

**Phase 3 found opportunities that score modestly higher than the previous winners. It did not find one that is proven better.**

- Under one consistent rubric, the top three new candidates beat the best previous idea (UK MTD prep, rescored at 52.6) by 2 to 7 points:
  - **Blender/VRChat pipeline add-ons:** 59.4
  - **Hobbyist sports-data API for college baseball:** 57.2
  - **Ready-to-run Meshtastic family kits:** 54.6
- In earlier rounds I treated gaps under 5 points as noise. A gap of about 7 is a lean, not a verdict.
- The improvement comes from **observable channels and low test cost.** These buyers already purchase through marketplaces where sales are visible: Superhive's $52.8M in creator payouts, Amazon "bought in past month" badges, Patreon paid-member counts.
- It does **not** come from stronger evidence that customers would buy our particular offer. That is still unverified for every candidate, new and old.

**Two caveats.**
1. **The rubric still favours software.** Criteria like low capital, high margin and operating leverage reward digital products automatically. The mesh-kit hardware idea still placing third partly offsets this.
2. **The higher-scoring ideas have lower ceilings.** The top-scoring new ideas (add-ons, sports API) are cheap to test and have proven channels, but their realistic earnings are mostly Track A. The new idea with the clearest Track B ceiling, the Spanish-language Holy Land box, scores lower because its acquisition cost and inventory needs are unverified.

## Deliverable 1: Lessons from Rounds 1–2

### Biases in how we looked

1. **We searched where evidence is easy to find.**
   - Regulatory deadlines, compliance, tax automation, migrations and enterprise workflows all leave public paper trails: government pages, vendor blogs and law-firm notes.
   - Agents found them quickly and could "verify" them, so they dominated both rounds (roughly 70% of candidates).
   - That reflected the method, not commercial superiority.
2. **The scoring rewarded deadlines.** Twenty of 100 cash-flow points went to "urgency", which effectively meant "is there a deadline".
3. **Competitor discovery was treated as fatal.** The red team killed or downgraded most ideas the moment a competitor appeared. But those competitors were evidence that customers spend money in that space.
4. **Distribution was assessed last.** Ideas were scored on their problem and their evidence, and the question of how buyers would be reached was weighted lightly.
5. **We ran out of search capacity.** In Round 2 the search quota ran out early, so "no competitor found" often meant "not searched". A snippet also misattributed a price to an unrelated Kenyan firm.
6. **Contracting looked like a business.** The Round 1 winner, Atlassian Forge work, was skilled contracting, and the rubric did not penalise dependence on the founder's own labour enough.

### Areas Rounds 1–2 missed

- Consumer and prosumer hobbies
- Physical products
- Enthusiast and creator ecosystems paid through Patreon or marketplaces
- Diaspora markets and markets where buyers value authentic goods from Israel
- Local Israeli services aimed at non-business customers
- Hardware crowdfunding as a sales channel
- Data products for hobbyists

### What changed in Phase 3

- The urgency criterion was removed.
- A separate distribution score was added, worth 20% of the balanced score.
- Labour dependence was scored explicitly.
- Competitors were checked first, but used as evidence of spending rather than as automatic rejection.
- Each agent had a hard search budget.

## Deliverable 4: Five deep-dive finalists

**How I chose them:** each is among the highest scorers after audit (section 6), the red team let each survive, and together they cover different business models (digital marketplace, data subscription, hardware, consumer subscription, service bridge).

**Left out:**
- **H10:** Ketubah.com already operates the exact model at scale.
- **H5:** markets too small.
- **H4:** I kept it as the runner-up. Its chosen niche has no demand evidence, though the category is strong.

### F1: Blender / VRChat pipeline add-ons (H7)

- **Product:** A small portfolio of paid add-ons, each fixing a "seam" between Blender and another tool. Examples:
  - Blender → 3D-print preflight (wall thickness, print-in-place clearances)
  - Blender → Roblox/VRChat/Unreal export fixers
  - Avatar setup automation

  Priced $15–40 one-time, with paid major upgrades.
- **Ideal customer:** Hobbyist and indie 3D creators who already buy add-ons on Superhive, Gumroad or Booth.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Superhive has paid creators $52,795,662 for 5,729,017 items sold to 1,125,519 customers [Verified, superhivemarket.com/market-stats].
  - Its 2023 Cyber Sale paid $632,806 in 4 days [Verified snippet].
  - Poiyomi (VRChat shaders) has 10,816 paid patrons, +300 in 30 days [Verified, Graphtreon].
  - **Limit:** sales per product are hidden, so a typical add-on's revenue is unknown.
- **Alternatives:** 5,910 existing add-ons, free tools, Blender's own core features, and doing it by hand.
- **How to enter:**
  - Target workflows *between* ecosystems, which Blender's core team won't build.
  - Use the founder's strength (fast, AI-assisted development) for speed of updates and polish.
  - Release a tutorial video alongside each add-on.
- **Customer acquisition:**
  - Superhive storefront search and site-wide sale events, a channel with observed purchases.
  - YouTube demos, r/blender, BlenderArtists, VRChat Discords.
- **Pricing:** $15–40.
- **Unit economics** [Hypothesis]:
  - About $17.50 net per $25 sale. This assumes roughly a 70% creator share, which is an older figure I haven't verified.
  - Direct costs are near zero. Founder time is about 3–4 weeks per add-on, plus maintenance with each Blender release.
  - **Downside:** 10 sales/mo → ~$175/mo
  - **Base:** 80 sales/mo across 3 add-ons → ~$1.4k/mo
  - **Upside:** 300/mo → ~$5.2k/mo
  - Owner profit is roughly equal to these figures, because there's no cost of goods.
- **Scalability:** Revenue isn't tied to labour once shipped. Track A is realistic over 12+ months; Track B only with a hit.
- **Risks:**
  - Hit-driven sales.
  - Blender absorbing the feature into its core ("sherlocking").
  - Discoverability among thousands of add-ons.
  - Hostility toward AI-made tools in creator communities (don't market it as AI).
- **Labour classification:** Scales beyond the founder's hours.

### F2: Hobbyist sports-data API for college baseball and softball (H6)

- **Product:** CFBD-style service: a free API key, $1–30/mo tiers on Patreon or a merchant of record, R/Python client packages, and a clean historical archive.
- **Ideal customer:** Amateur betting modellers, fantasy players and student analysts following college baseball.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - CFBD (college football, now also basketball) has 3,671 paid members, +705 in the last 30 days, estimated at $6–29k/mo [Verified, Graphtreon].
  - collegebaseballdata.com is a parked "Launching Soon" domain [Verified]. That suggests someone else spotted the gap.
  - Open-source scrapers exist: `collegebaseball` for Python.
- **Alternatives:**
  - Highlightly's commercial NCAA baseball API [Evidence, red team].
  - Free scrapers.
  - Enterprise feeds such as SportsDataIO.
  - Padel and women's college basketball were eliminated as targets because both already have paid APIs [Verified].
- **How to enter:** Hobbyist pricing, reliability, a clean archive and client packages, which is the CFBD playbook.
- **Customer acquisition:** r/CollegeBaseball analytics threads, the sports-analytics community on X/Bluesky, Kaggle datasets, CRAN/PyPI packages. CFBD's growth through community channels shows this route works.
- **Pricing:** Free / $3 / $10 / $25 per month.
- **Unit economics** [Hypothesis]:
  - Average about $5 per paying member; hosting $50–200/mo.
  - **Downside:** 50 paying → ~$250/mo
  - **Base:** 400 → ~$2k/mo
  - **Upside:** 1,000 → ~$5k/mo
  - A college-baseball audience is plausibly 5–10× smaller than college football's.
- **Scalability:** A single sport is a Track A ceiling. Track B would need several sports or B2B licences.
- **Risks:**
  - **Data rights (decisive):** NCAA terms of use are not verified.
  - Seasonality (February to June).
  - The holder of the parked domain may launch first.
- **Labour classification:** Scales beyond the founder's hours, with in-season maintenance.

### F3: Ready-to-run Meshtastic family/neighbourhood kits (H1)

- **Product:** A kit of 3 pre-paired handhelds plus 1 solar repeater (US 915 MHz). Units come pre-flashed with a private channel, in weatherproof housings with the correct mounting hardware, plus QR-code onboarding, a printed quick-start card, and US-hours support. Price $349–449.
- **Ideal customer:** Emergency-preparedness households, hiking and overlanding groups, and rural neighbourhoods. Hurricane and wildfire regions first.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - NomadNode, a small brand, sells a pre-flashed node on Amazon at $47.99 with 4.9★ from 19 ratings and "200+ bought in past month" [Verified].
  - Amazon shows the node "frequently bought together" with a T1000-E, so buyers already purchase several nodes.
  - Muzi and Rokland items are sold out [Verified].
  - Higher Amazon volume figures (P1-Pro 400+/mo, Wio 500+/mo) came from a browser check and were not re-verified.
- **Alternatives:**
  - RAK and Seeed single devices ($39–109) and DIY starter kits.
  - Seeed's $1,929 kit for professional responders.
  - **The audit found no family multi-pack** at RAK, Seeed, Muzi or Rokland.
- **How to enter:** Sell an outcome ("family comms when cell towers are down") rather than a board. Pre-pairing, support and the onboarding app are the founder's software edge.
- **Customer acquisition:** Amazon search (demonstrably converts in this category), YouTube preparedness creators, local mesh Discords, and seasonal pushes before hurricane season.
- **Pricing:** $399 kit.
- **Unit economics** [Hypothesis]:
  - Hardware cost of goods about $180 (~45%); Amazon referral fee ~$60; FBA and hazmat handling ~$20.
  - **Contribution about $140 per kit before ads.**
  - **Downside:** 5 kits/mo → $700
  - **Base:** 30 → ~$4.2k
  - **Upside:** 100 → ~$14k

  Ad costs are unknown.
- **Capital:** Twenty kits cost about $3.6k in hardware, which exceeds the budget. Run a presale or landing page first, then a hand-assembled batch of 20.
- **Scalability:** Contract kitting at a US 3PL plus scripted provisioning makes this scalable. Support grows with units sold.
- **Risks:**
  - RAK (which already has a "Crisis Alert" category) launches family packs.
  - Paperwork for lithium-battery shipping (UN3481) and FBA hazmat review.
  - Returns from non-technical buyers.
  - Customs and tariffs on goods into the US.
  - The trend may fade.
- **Labour classification:** Mixed. Partly labour-dependent early on; scalable once kitting is outsourced.

### F4: Spanish-language Holy Land subscription box (H8)

- **Product:** A quarterly box of Israeli artisan and biblical items, built natively in Spanish: story cards, a devotional, video from the land, Spanish customer support, and a WhatsApp community. Seasonal gift boxes too.
- **Ideal customer:** US Hispanic evangelical households first; Spanish-speaking Latin America later.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Artza: $94 per box, claims 85k customers and 185k+ boxes [Verified, self-reported].
  - Lev Haolam: $119/mo, **2,334 Trustpilot reviews, 341 in the last 12 months** [Verified].
  - Both are English-only, with no Spanish or Portuguese competitor box found [Verified, absence-based].
- **Alternatives:** The English boxes, one-off Israeli e-shops with Spanish storefronts (e.g. peace-of-god.com), and church gift shops.
- **How to enter:** Copy the proven format and own the Spanish-language identity. Source from inside the Green Line to limit activist exposure.
- **Customer acquisition:** Spanish Meta ads targeting evangelical interests, pastor and church affiliate programmes, and Spanish worship-media influencers. The incumbents built their scale direct-to-consumer, but **the Spanish-language acquisition cost is unknown**.
- **Pricing:** $69–79 per box.
- **Unit economics** [Hypothesis]:
  - Goods $20–28, packaging $5, shipping plus duty $18–25 (tariff now about 12.5% under Section 301 per a secondary source, and litigated), payment fees $3.
  - **Contribution about $20–30 per box before acquisition cost.**
  - **Downside:** 50 subscribers → ~$400/mo
  - **Base:** 500 → ~$4k/mo minus acquisition cost
  - **Upside:** 3,000 → ~$25k/mo
- **Capital:** About $3–4k for 100 boxes, so a founding-member presale is required.
- **Scalability:** The clearest Track B among the new ideas. An Israeli fulfilment house would remove founder labour.
- **Risks:**
  - **Acquisition cost versus contribution (decisive).**
  - Incumbents adding Spanish.
  - War-related delays.
  - Tariff swings.
  - Hispanic evangelicals' affinity for Israel may be weaker than assumed.
- **Labour classification:** Scales beyond the founder's hours once fulfilment is outsourced; sourcing needs ongoing curation.

### F5: Remote b'nai mitzvah preparation with Israeli tutors and an AI practice tool (H9)

- **Product:** A fixed-price, 6–9 month package: live Zoom lessons with vetted Israel-based tutors, plus an app that scores the student's chanting practice against reference audio between lessons. Optional ceremony in Israel through partners.
- **Ideal customer:** Unaffiliated or non-Orthodox Jewish families in the UK, Europe, Australia and South Africa (good time-zone fit), and US families on Sundays.

- **Pricing:** $1,950.

  - 24 tutor hours at about $35/hr = $840, leaving **about $1,100 gross per student** before acquisition cost.

  - **Upside:** 12 → ~$13k (needs a larger tutor network)

- **Risks:**
  - Trust in an unknown online provider.
  - Acquisition cost.
  - One-time purchase per child.
  - US time zones.
  - Tutor quality control.

## Deliverable 8: The three most promising new discoveries

### Best quick-profit opportunity: Remote b'nai mitzvah prep (H9)

- **Why:** Each sale earns about $1,100 gross. There's no inventory, startup cost is under $2k, and the founder isn't the one delivering lessons. Market prices of $3.6–6.5k leave room to undercut.
- **Most important assumption to validate:** Unaffiliated families in the UK, Europe or Australia will pay about $2k up front to an unknown, Israel-based online provider, and they can be reached through search or rabbi referrals at an acquisition cost of a few hundred dollars per family.

### Best scalable opportunity: Spanish-language Holy Land box (H8)

- **Why:** It's the only new idea with a verified category of large recurring subscriptions: an incumbent with 341 reviews in the last 12 months, and claims of 85k and 30k customers. It has a confirmed language gap, sourcing in Israel is a genuine advantage, and fulfilment can be outsourced.
- **Most important assumption to validate:** Spanish-speaking evangelicals will subscribe at about $69–79 per box, at an acquisition cost recovered within about 2 boxes ($40–60 per subscriber).

### Best risk-adjusted opportunity: Blender / VRChat pipeline add-ons (H7)

- **Why:** It has the highest evidence-adjusted score. Capital needed is about $0, the marketplace has verified purchase volume, the founder's skills fit best, there's no shipping, tariff or boycott exposure, and failure costs only weeks.
- **Most important assumption to validate:** A specific pipeline add-on, such as a Blender-to-print preflight, gets 30 or more paid sales in its first 60 days on Superhive or Gumroad.

**Runner-up worth noting:** H1, the Meshtastic kits. It has the best physical-product evidence (no multi-packs on the market, sellouts, and a new brand at 200+/mo). Its decisive assumption: buyers will pay about $399 for a pre-paired kit that is about $250 in parts.
